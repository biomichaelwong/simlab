! DSE Key Concept Notes:

1. Intrapleural Pressure (Purple Line)

  • It is always negative (below atmospheric pressure). This slight vacuum keeps the lungs adhered to the chest wall, preventing lung collapse.
  • During inhalation, as the thoracic cavity expands, intrapleural pressure drops further (from –4 to –8 mm Hg).

2. Sequence of Events (Inhalation)

  1. Intercostal muscles and diaphragm contract.
  2. Ribs move upwards and outwards, while the diaphragm flattens, causing thoracic cavity volume to increase.
  3. Intrapleural pressure drops, pulling the lungs outward.
  4. Lung volume increases.
  5. Alveolar pressure drops below atmospheric pressure (deep red line dips below 0).
  6. Air is drawn into the lungs down the pressure gradient.
